Sharing One HF Antenna Between a Kenwood TS-590 and an RX-888 MKII Using the QRO.cz RX Audite SDR Switch

A practical station integration note. Revision 1.

Abstract

The RX-888 MKII is a direct-sampling 16-bit SDR with an SMA antenna input that connects to the ADC almost directly. Its absolute maximum RF input is about +10 dBm (10 mW) and clipping begins well below that; even brief exposure to transmitter RF at the watt level will destroy it. The QRO.cz RX Audite SDR Switch is purpose-built for this scenario: it shares one transmit/receive antenna between a transceiver and an SDR, mutes and clamps the SDR port during transmit, and provides high voltage, surge, and overcurrent protection. This article describes the topology, the cabling bill of materials with order links, the configuration of both the Kenwood TS-590 and the RX-888 MKII, and a smoke-test commissioning sequence designed so that no failure mode short of catastrophic operator error puts full TX power on the SDR.

2. Why a Bare Tee or Diode-Pair Splitter Is Not Acceptable

The most common failures of receive-only SDRs in shared-antenna installations fall into three buckets. First, the operator transmits with the SDR still connected directly to the antenna; even 5 W into an unprotected SMA port will destroy the front end of an RX-888. Second, a nearby station or a lightning-induced surge couples energy in through the antenna while the radio is off; back-to-back diode pads (the protection found in cheap "SDR splitters") will conduct but cannot dissipate sustained energy and have nothing in series to limit it. Third, common-mode currents on the coax cause RF to enter the SDR through its USB shield, which the antenna-side splitter cannot fix.

The RX Audite addresses all three. It uses a relay-switched signal path commanded by the transceiver's PTT line, so during transmit the SDR port is physically disconnected from the antenna and routed instead either to an internal dummy load or to a separate INBAND RX antenna port. In addition, a soft-clipping RF limiter rated for sustained input sits on the SDR port at all times, supplemented by an over-voltage clamp ( > 60 V AC ) and a resettable current "SuperFuse" (> 100 mA). When the unit is unpowered or its front-panel switch is OFF, the antenna is routed to the SDR through these protections only, which is the safe failure mode for a desktop accessory.

3. Recommended Topology

For a station with one HF antenna, one TS-590, and one RX-888 MKII, the recommended wiring is a single-line block: antenna into the Audite, transceiver hung off the Audite's TRX port, SDR hung off the Audite's SDR RX port, and a PTT line from the TS-590 REMOTE jack into the Audite's PTT IN. The TS-590 RX ANT IN/OUT loop is left unused in this baseline; section 6 describes an optional second hardware mute that uses it.

5. TS-590 REMOTE Jack PTT Wiring

The TS-590 REMOTE jack is a 7-pin DIN connector on the rear panel. The full pinout, taken from the Kenwood TS-590S Instruction Manual ("Connecting Peripheral Equipment", REMOTE CONNECTOR table), is:

PinNameFunctionI/O
1SPOSpeaker outputO
2COMCommon terminal (signal ground for the relay contacts on pins 4 and 5)I/O
3SSStandby input: when externally grounded, the transceiver enters TX modeI
4MKEInternal control relay: connected to COM (pin 2) when the linear amplifier is keyed for TXI/O
5BRKInternal control relay: connected to COM (pin 2) during RXI/O
6ALCALC input from the amplifier (approximately −7 V)I
7RLApproximately +12 V DC output during TX, 10 mA maxO

The line we want is pin 4 (MKE), returned through pin 2 (COM). During TX an internal mechanical relay shorts pin 4 to pin 2; during RX the relay is open. This is exactly the "open = RX, ground = TX" signal the Audite PTT IN expects. The Kenwood manual states it directly: "For amplifiers that enter TX mode when the control terminal is grounded, connect pin 2 of the REMOTE connector to the GND terminal of the amplifier and connect pin 4 of the connector to the control terminal of the amplifier." The Audite is electrically identical to such an amplifier from the radio's point of view.

Wire pin 4 to the RCA center pin and pin 2 to the RCA shell with shielded two-conductor cable; tie the cable shield to pin 2 only at the TS-590 end to avoid a ground loop. Do not use pin 7 (RL): it sources +12 V during TX, which is the wrong polarity for the Audite's active-low PTT input and will simply leave the switch in RX during transmit. Do not use pin 3 (SS) either: that pin is an input that causes the radio to transmit when grounded, not an output that reports its TX state.

Required menu setting. The MKE/BRK relay is only driven if the TS-590 linear amplifier control relay is enabled. Set Menu 53 (HF LINEAR AMPLIFIER CONTROL RELAY) to 2 (standard) or 3 (slow, for amplifiers that need extra keying delay). The default of OFF leaves pin 4 unconnected and the Audite will never see PTT. For 6 m operation also set Menu 54 the same way.

Verify before connecting. Before plugging the cable into the Audite, check the wiring with a multimeter. With the radio off and the cable unplugged from the Audite, you should read open circuit between RCA tip and RCA shell. With the radio on, Menu 53 set to 2, and the radio in RX, you should still read open. Press PTT into a dummy load (or hold MOX with the antenna disconnected) and the meter should read continuity (a few ohms or less) tip-to-shell. If the polarity is reversed, the cable is wired wrong; rework before connecting it to the Audite.

PTT timing and break-in. The Audite specifies a minimum PTT lead/tail of 20 ms. The TS-590's MKE relay timing in the standard linear-amp setting is comfortably above this, but full break-in (FBK) CW will not give the Audite enough lead time and will hot-switch its relay. Use semi break-in (SBK) or longer for CW, and avoid VOX with very short hang times. The Audite is not a QSK device.

6. Optional Belt-and-Braces: Use the TS-590 RX ANT Loop as a Second Hardware Mute

The TS-590 has an RX ANT IN/OUT pair on RCA jacks that lets you insert a filter or splitter into the receive path. When TX is active, the TS-590 internally disconnects this loop. Routing the SDR through this loop in series with the Audite gives a second, completely independent hardware disconnect: even if the Audite's relay welded shut and its limiter failed simultaneously, the TS-590's own TX/RX switch would still mute the SDR side. The cost is two more RCA-to-PL-259 adapters and the loss of the ability to use the SDR while the TS-590 is off (because the TS-590 then opens the loop).

For the level of paranoia implied by "I MUST NOT fry the SDR," the baseline single-Audite topology in section 3 is sufficient. The TS-590 RX ANT loop trick is most useful if the SDR is also being used as a panadapter that follows the TS-590's IF or first mixer, which is a different configuration outside the scope of this note.

8. Commissioning Sequence (Smoke Test)

Do not skip steps. Each one is a checkpoint that, if it fails, is recoverable; subsequent steps are not.

StepActionPass criterion
1Power off everything. Wire the system per Figure 1, but with a 50 ohm dummy load on the Audite "Main TX/RX ANT" port and the antenna disconnected.All connectors finger-tight, station ground bonded, USB to RX-888 not yet connected.
2Apply 13.8 V to the Audite. Power on the TS-590 only. Front-panel POWER switch on the Audite OFF.Audite DC LED on, no PTT LED, no smoke.
3Connect USB to the RX-888. Open the SDR application. Confirm noise floor on a quiet HF frequency.Receiver sees the dummy load (very low noise floor, no signals).
4Turn the Audite POWER switch ON. Confirm SDR still works through the live signal path with the limiter inline.Noise floor unchanged or slightly different; no oscillation.
5Set the TS-590 to 5 W output, CW mode, into the dummy load. Tap the key briefly.Audite PTT LED lights on key-down. SDR application shows the limiter clamping (a short, contained event), not a runaway level. No fault.
6Disconnect USB, raise the TS-590 to 100 W, key down for 2 seconds into the dummy load with the Audite live.No heat from the Audite SDR port, no relay chatter, no fault. Reconnect USB and verify the SDR still enumerates and receives normally.
7Replace the dummy load with the real antenna. Repeat step 5 at low power on a quiet portion of a band you have permission to use.Same behavior as step 5 with the antenna in line.
8Resume normal operating power.System ready.

If at any step the SDR's noise floor jumps abnormally on key-down, stop. The most likely cause is a missing or wrong PTT connection: the Audite is not seeing PTT and the SDR port is being limited only by the diode clamp, which for a 100 W carrier produces a very loud spike at the SDR. Check Cable C, verify Menu 53 is set to 2 or 3, and re-run the multimeter polarity check from section 5 before going further.

10. Summary

The QRO.cz RX Audite SDR Switch is exactly the right device for sharing one HF antenna between a TS-590 and an RX-888 MKII without risking the SDR. The wiring is one PL-259 jumper from Audite TRX to TS-590 ANT1, one SMA jumper from Audite SDR RX OUT to the RX-888 MKII, one homebrew or off-the-shelf REMOTE-DIN-to-RCA PTT cable wired pin 4 (MKE) to RCA tip and pin 2 (COM) to RCA shell, with TS-590 Menu 53 set to 2 or 3, and one DC supply. Both PL-259 and SMA jumpers can be ordered ready-made from the Audite vendor (HamParts.shop) so that the entire signal path from antenna to ADC uses connectors that someone other than the operator has crimped. With the smoke-test sequence in section 8 followed in order, no single failure short of forgetting to plug in the PTT line will put more than a few milliwatts on the SDR's SMA input.
